# An upper bound for the LRX diameter

Let $D_n$ be the diameter of the Cayley graph of $S_n$ generated by the operations

$$
\begin{aligned}
L[x_0,x_1,\ldots,x_{n-1}]&=[x_1,\ldots,x_{n-1},x_0],\\
R&=L^{-1},\\
X[x_0,x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_{n-1}]&=[x_1,x_0,x_2,\ldots,x_{n-1}].
\end{aligned}
$$

Each letter costs one operation. Words are executed **from left to right**. Positions and labels are identified with $\mathbb Z_n$, and

$$
\delta_n(a,b)=\min\bigl((b-a)\bmod n,(a-b)\bmod n\bigr).
$$

**Theorem.** For every $n\ge4$, putting $P=\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor$,

$$
\boxed{
D_n\le 2P+n-\left\lceil\frac{2P+11n-11}{3n}\right\rceil
\le \binom n2+\left\lfloor\frac{4n}{3}\right\rfloor-3.
}
$$

It suffices to sort every permutation within this bound: the Cayley graph is vertex-transitive, and the generating set is closed under inverses.

## 1. Paths and edge loads for one cycle

Fix a nontrivial cycle $C=(a_0,\ldots,a_{k-1})$, with indices read modulo $k$. Replace each arrow $a_j\to a_{j+1}$ by a shortest path on the circle of positions. Choose either direction in a tie. Write $d_j\ne0$ for its signed length, positive in the direction $i\to i+1$, and set

$$
F=\sum_j|d_j|,\qquad
h_i=\#\{\text{chosen paths using the edge }\{i,i+1\}\},\qquad
M=\max_i h_i.
$$

Thus $F=\sum_i h_i$. Let $t$ count the cyclic sign changes $-\to+$. There are also $t$ changes $+\to-$, so

$$
E=k-2t\ge0.
$$

At a cycle vertex $i=a_j$, the incoming and outgoing paths give

$$
h_i-h_{i-1}=
\begin{cases}
2,&d_{j-1}<0<d_j,\\
-2,&d_{j-1}>0>d_j,\\
0,&d_{j-1},d_j\text{ have the same sign}.
\end{cases}
\tag{1}
$$

Paths passing through $i$ contribute equally to both adjacent edges. At vertices outside $C$, the difference is zero.

If both signs occur, choose a vertex $b$ where the load rises to its maximum: $h_b=M$ and the sign change is $-\to+$. Such a vertex exists by taking the start of a maximal plateau. If all signs agree, the load is constant, and any vertex of $C$ may serve as $b$.

The $t$ distinct edges immediately preceding rises have load at most $M-2$. Consequently,

$$
nM-F\ge2t,
\qquad\boxed{F+k\le nM+E.}
\tag{2}
$$

## 2. Realizing a cycle with a carrier

Rotate the array so that position $b$ is first. Keep this first slot as a **carrier**, and regard the other $n-1$ slots as a ring. The identities

$$
\begin{aligned}
XL[x_0,x_1,\ldots,x_{n-1}]
&=[x_0,x_2,\ldots,x_{n-1},x_1],\\
RX[x_0,x_1,\ldots,x_{n-1}]
&=[x_0,x_{n-1},x_1,\ldots,x_{n-2}]
\end{aligned}
$$

move this ring by one step in either direction while preserving the carrier's content. Each step costs two letters.

Start the cycle notation at $a_0=b$. Move the ring to bring slot $a_1$ next to the carrier and apply $X$; repeat with $a_2,\ldots,a_{k-1}$, then return the ring to its initial orientation. These are the successive exchanges

$$
(b,a_1),(b,a_2),\ldots,(b,a_{k-1}).
$$

The resulting array index permutation is $C^{-1}$, and all slots outside $C$ are restored.

### Movement cost

Identify $b$ with $b+1$, contracting the edge $\{b,b+1\}$. The remaining circle is exactly the ring of $n-1$ slots, initially ordered $b+1,b+2,\ldots,b-1$. The contracted point represents its initial slot next to the carrier.

Project the chosen paths onto this ring, retaining their directions. Every traversal of the contracted edge removes one step; other steps are unchanged. Following the projected paths visits the required slots in order and returns to the starting point, hence restores the ring's orientation. The total number of ring steps is therefore

$$
A=F-h_b=F-M.
\tag{3}
$$

Before cancellations, the internal word has length $2A+k-1$.

### Disjoint cancellations

At each exchange, an incoming negative segment ends in $X$, and an outgoing positive segment starts in $X$. Thus either gives an adjacent pair $XX$, removable because $X^2=1$:

$$
(RX)^rX\quad(r\ge1),\qquad X(XL)^s\quad(s\ge1).
$$

An exchange lacks both opportunities only at a sign change $+\to-$. All $t$ such changes occur away from $b$. Hence at least $k-1-t$ exchanges admit a cancellation.

These cancellations can be chosen disjointly: choose one at each eligible exchange. A positive segment can supply its first $X$ only to the preceding exchange; a negative segment can supply its last $X$ only to the following exchange. No segment supplies letters to both.

Contraction can make a segment empty only when its original path is the single contracted edge. This can occur only for the first positive segment or the last negative segment; neither supplies a counted cancellation. This also covers the case when all signs agree.

Thus the internal word $W_C$, excluding the initial positioning rotation and any later external rotations, satisfies

$$
\begin{aligned}
|W_C|
&\le 2(F-M)+k-1-2(k-1-t)\\
&=2F-S(C),\qquad
\boxed{S(C)=2M+E-1.}
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$

## 3. A uniform estimate for the saving

We claim

$$
\boxed{3nS(C)\ge5(F+k).}
\tag{5}
$$

By (2),

$$
\begin{aligned}
3nS(C)-5(F+k)
&\ge3n(2M+E-1)-5(nM+E)\\
&=n(M-3)+(3n-5)E.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$

This is nonnegative for $M\ge3$. Since $F>0$, only the following cases remain.

- **$M=1$.** Equation (1) forbids sign changes, so $E=k\ge2$. The right-hand side of (6) is at least $4n-10>0$.
- **$M=2$, $E\ge1$.** The right-hand side of (6) is at least $2n-5>0$.
- **$M=2$, $E=0$.** Signs alternate. By (1), all loads have the same parity; since the maximum is 2, they are 0 or 2. A sign change ensures that some load is zero. The union of the paths is connected, because each path ends where the next begins. Its support is therefore one proper arc of the circle. There is exactly one rise from 0 to 2, so $t=1$ and $k=2t=2$. This is a transposition, with $S(C)=3$ and $F=2\delta_n(a_0,a_1)\le n$. Hence $3nS(C)-5(F+k)\ge4n-10>0$.

This proves (5).

## 4. Combining cycles and rotations

Represent the input array by the bijection $\pi:i\mapsto x_i$, and let $\lambda(i)=i+1\pmod n$. An index permutation $g$ changes the array to $\pi\circ g$; in particular, $L$ has index permutation $\lambda$. Thus chronological execution of $g$, then $h$, changes it to $\pi\circ g\circ h$.

For each $c\in\mathbb Z_n$, put

$$
f_c=\lambda^c\circ\pi,\qquad
F_c=\sum_i\delta_n(i,f_c(i)),\qquad
N_c=\#\{i:f_c(i)\ne i\}.
$$

Realizing $f_c^{-1}$, then $L^c$, sorts the input because

$$
\pi\circ f_c^{-1}\circ\lambda^c=\mathrm{id}.
\tag{7}
$$

Decompose $f_c$ into disjoint nontrivial cycles. By (4)–(5), the sum of their internal word lengths is at most

$$
\left(2-\frac5{3n}\right)F_c-\frac5{3n}N_c.
\tag{8}
$$

Let $b_1,\ldots,b_K$ be their chosen carrier positions. Since the cycle permutations commute, their order is arbitrary. Combining the positioning rotations gives a word of the form

$$
L^{b_1}W_1L^{b_2-b_1}W_2\cdots
L^{b_K-b_{K-1}}W_KL^{c-b_K},
$$

where negative powers use $R$. The external cost is bounded by a walk on the circle from 0 to $c$ visiting all carrier positions; it suffices to visit all vertices.

Such a walk has length at most

$$
H(c)=
\begin{cases}
n,&c=0,\\
n+\delta_n(0,c)-2,&c\ne0.
\end{cases}
\tag{9}
$$

For $c=0$, take a full circuit. Otherwise choose a shortest direction from 0 to $c$, and let $v$ be the first neighbor in that direction. Go the other way around the circle from 0 to $v$, using $n-1$ edges and visiting every vertex; then go from $v$ to $c$ along the chosen shortest arc, using $\delta_n(0,c)-1$ edges.

Consequently, for every $c$,

$$
d(\pi,\mathrm{id})\le
\left(2-\frac5{3n}\right)F_c-\frac5{3n}N_c+H(c).
\tag{10}
$$

## 5. Averaging

The sum of distances from a vertex to all vertices of the circle is

$$
\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z_n}\delta_n(0,j)=\left\lfloor\frac{n^2}{4}\right\rfloor=P.
$$

For each fixed $i$, the values $f_c(i)$ run through all vertices as $c$ varies. Exactly one equals $i$. Therefore

$$
\frac1n\sum_cF_c=P,\qquad
\frac1n\sum_cN_c=n-1.
\tag{11}
$$

Equation (9) also gives

$$
\frac1n\sum_cH(c)=n-2+\frac{P+2}{n}.
\tag{12}
$$

Averaging (10), we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
d(\pi,\mathrm{id})
&\le\left(2-\frac5{3n}\right)P
-\frac5{3n}(n-1)+n-2+\frac{P+2}{n}\\
&=2P+n-\frac{2P+11n-11}{3n}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Taking the integer part proves the first bound in the theorem.

For completeness, the last real expression equals

$$
\binom n2+\frac{4n}{3}+
\begin{cases}
-\dfrac{11}{3}+\dfrac{11}{3n},&n\text{ even},\\[4pt]
-\dfrac{25}{6}+\dfrac{23}{6n},&n\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
$$

The correction is at most $-3$ for even $n\ge6$ and odd $n\ge5$. Taking integer parts gives the second bound. For $n=4$, both displayed integer bounds equal 8. This completes the proof.

## 6. Sharpness of the auxiliary estimate

The coefficient $5/3$ in $S(C)\ge\frac{5}{3n}(F+k)$ cannot be increased uniformly for this definition of $S(C)$.

Indeed, let $n=4r$, $r\ge2$, and take

$$
C_r=(0,\,2r-1,\,r,\,3r-1,\,2r,\,4r-1,\,3r,\,r-1).
$$

Its unique shortest paths alternate between lengths $+(2r-1)$ and $-(r-1)$. Thus

$$
k=8,\quad t=4,\quad E=0,\quad F=3n-8.
$$

There are rises at $0,r,2r,3r$ and falls at $r-1,2r-1,3r-1,4r-1$. By (1), the loads equal some $m$ on those four latter edges and $m+2$ elsewhere. Their sum is $F=3n-8$, so $nm+2(n-4)=3n-8$, giving $m=1$. Hence $M=3$, and

$$
S(C_r)=5=\frac5{3n}(F+k).
$$

This establishes sharpness only of this auxiliary saving estimate. It does not establish optimality of the constructed words or the diameter bound, and does not rule out improvements using other shifts, constructions, or additional cancellations.
